PhD in Deep Learning

Hi, I’m Santiago, a Data Scientist and AI Engineer based in Paris. I hold a PhD in Deep Learning, where I built graph neural networks and large-scale ML pipelines for a particle-physics experiment (KM3NeT). Over the past 5+ years I’ve focused on production-grade ML where I trained several deep learning models (in particular Graph Neural Networks), processing 10+ TB datasets on distributed GPU/HPC infrastructure, and cutting preprocessing time by 40% through parallelization and I/O optimization. My core stack is Python, PyTorch, SQL, Docker, AWS, MLflow and CI/CD, with hands-on work in LLM/NLP/RAG workflows, I also use C++ and Julia. I’ve co-led an international team of a dozen of researchers and taught Python and algorithms at Université Paris Cité. A landmark discovery in neutrino astronomy — Featured on the cover of Nature

I am proud to have contributed to the international KM3NeT Collaboration behind the discovery of an extraordinary cosmic neutrino, reported in our paper “Observation of an ultra-high-energy cosmic neutrino with KM3NeT,” published in Nature in February 2025. This work reports the observation of an exceptionally energetic cosmic neutrino, associated with an event designated KM3-230213A. With an estimated energy far exceeding that of previously observed neutrinos, this remarkable event opens a new window onto the most extreme astrophysical phenomena in the Universe. It raises exciting questions about the origins of ultra-high-energy cosmic rays and the powerful cosmic accelerators capable of producing such particles. The discovery was made using KM3NeT, a large underwater neutrino telescope in the Mediterranean Sea. It demonstrates the extraordinary potential of detecting neutrinos through the faint Cherenkov light produced by secondary charged particles travelling through seawater, allowing us to explore some of the most energetic and distant processes in the cosmos. A particularly special recognition of this work was its selection for the front cover of Nature (13 February 2025), featuring the KM3NeT detector under the headline “Cosmic Catcher”. Seeing this collaborative scientific achievement highlighted on the cover of one of the world’s leading scientific journals was a truly rewarding moment. I am delighted to have been part of this international effort, bringing together scientists and engineers from across the world to push the boundaries of neutrino astronomy and our understanding of the Universe. My contributions to the paper where the statistical analyses for significance of background vs signal.
